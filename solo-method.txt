Solo uses one player, 20 rounds, and a final target of 2,335. The target remains fixed for the entire game. Total rounds and search settings are disabled in Solo because the included strategy is the fixed 20-round solution. Your banked score can be edited under Players or in Set state. Dice entry, automatic doubles, BANK, next round and Undo use the same controls as multiplayer.

The Solo action uses the full integer-state Bellman policy previously calculated, including nonmonotone stopping intervals where present. It does not use a neural network, Monte Carlo action selection, a fixed pot multiplier or a score-grid approximation. Its compact data was compared against all 54,498,900 source decisions needed for the 2,335 target. Floating-point arithmetic evaluates probabilities numerically; the probability display rounds to two decimal places. The optimal initial target probability is approximately 50.0194765%. The objective is the largest final-score target attainable at least half the time, not maximum arithmetic mean points. Reaching 2,335 is not guaranteed.

The relevant state is the current pot, total banked points, and rounds remaining including the current round. Start by banking at pot 913 or higher. After a first-round bust, the threshold is 966 with 19 rounds left. After banking 1,000 in round one, it is 252 with 19 rounds left. These are examples, not a replacement for the full lookup. The last safe starting pot is not separately needed once the current pot is known.

Solo percentages mean: chance of reaching 2,335 by the end of this game if you BANK now, versus if you CONTINUE now, followed by optimal decisions. They are counterfactual alternatives and do not add to 100%. Once the target is secured, Solo banks after the three safe rolls to continue adding points.

The Bellman recurrence is V(r,d,p)=max(F(r-1,d-p), F(r-1,d)/6+V(r,d,2p)/6+sum(n[s]*V(r,d,p+s))/36). Here r is remaining rounds, d is additional points needed, and p is current pot. The ordinary sums are 3,4,5,6,8,9,10,11, with counts 2,2,4,4,4,4,2,2 out of 36. F averages V over the exact distribution of the first three safe rolls; F(0,d>0)=0 and completed targets have value 1. The data stores F and the solved decisions for every remaining target up to 2,335 and every remaining round count through 20.

Multiplayer target policy
In a 20-round multiplayer game, select Solo target · multiplayer (20 rounds) under Advice engine to use the identical fixed 2,335-target strategy. It ignores opponent scores; it is exact only for the personal target objective, not for winning against rivals. Its percentages are target-reaching probabilities, not multiplayer win shares. Solo mode remains identical. Monte Carlo stays the default; Compare win search checks the current position using the multiplayer win objective.

Tournament
The included benchmark folder contains 70 completed 20-round games with six released search snapshots and the identical solo-target policy at one seven-player table. Each strategy occupies each seat ten times. Solo-target won 16 games and v3.5 won 14. This two-game margin is not strong evidence of superiority. Searches requested 100 ms soft time per decision; actual mean times were about 145–291 ms due to batch overruns. This is not a four-second, six-worker phone benchmark. The neural lab was excluded because no fixed trained phone checkpoint was supplied. Full raw records, measured times, source hashes and reproduction scripts are included.
